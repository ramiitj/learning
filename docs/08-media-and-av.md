# 08 — Media and Audio-Visual Content

## Principle

Media serves interaction; it never replaces it. The platform must not drift into a video library with quizzes.

## Rules (drawn from multimedia-learning research)

Video and audio segments are short (about one to three minutes) and followed by something the learner does. Narration accompanies graphics rather than on-screen text reading aloud what is written. Anything decorative that does not carry the concept is removed. What to look at is highlighted as it is discussed. Nothing autoplays.

## Interactive media

`video` blocks support pause points that ask for a prediction before continuing. `animation` blocks can be scrubbed by the learner and can respond to lesson state. `audio` narration can accompany an interaction step by step. Animated diagrams (SVG, Lottie, Rive) are preferred over filmed video because they are lighter, scale to any screen, translate cleanly and can respond to input. Filmed video is reserved for what genuinely needs it: a real place, a real person, a real lab.

## Delivery

Adaptive streaming at multiple resolutions; a low-bandwidth option; an audio-only or animated-diagram fallback; inclusion in offline lesson packs at a compressed resolution.

## Accessibility

Captions and transcripts in every published locale for every video and audio block; audio description where visuals carry meaning not otherwise available.

## Generated and third-party media

Claude writes scripts, storyboards, narration text, captions and SVG or animated diagrams directly. Images, voice and video from generation services are connected through the media pipeline. Every asset records its source (generated, commissioned, stock, creator's own), the generating service or author, its licence, and its review status. Nothing is published until the creator and the Child Safety reviewer have approved it.

## Cost awareness

Video storage and bandwidth dominate media cost. Animated diagrams and narrated interactions are the default for both pedagogical and financial reasons.
