# Reference: framing AI without giving it a mind

Used by: accuracy-safety-reviewer, curriculum-instructional-designer, analogy-specialist, localisation-specialist. Constitution Article 7.

Learners aged 13-17 readily believe AI systems understand, intend and remember. Every lesson must leave them with the opposite: systems find patterns in data and follow rules people chose.

## Flag these when applied to an AI system (any locale)

understands, knows, thinks, believes, wants, decides to (protect/help), cares, feels, is trying to, lies, is honest, is smart/clever (as a mind), remembers you, learns about you, gets to know you, is curious, is confused (as a feeling), means, intends.

Also flag: "AI is always right", "AI is objective/neutral", "AI learns from everything you type" (unless the lesson shows a system that does, and says so), "the machine taught itself" without saying from what data.

## Allowed

- Mechanistic verbs: counts, scores, compares, finds a pattern, matches, follows a rule, calculates, predicts (as "makes a guess from patterns"), sorts, is trained on.
- Human metaphors **inside a break-point discussion** that says where the metaphor stops working ("A child understands what a dog is. The machine has only found a pattern.").
- Quoting what people say ("people often say the AI 'knows'...") when the lesson then corrects it.

## Severity

Blocking when the lesson's own explanation relies on the mind-framing, or when the takeaway/ending states it. Advisory for a single loose verb in an aside that the lesson elsewhere corrects.
