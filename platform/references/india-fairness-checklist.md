# Reference: fairness and representation checklist (India, ages 13-17)

Used by: accuracy-safety-reviewer, curriculum-instructional-designer, analogy-specialist, localisation-specialist, educator-enablement. Constitution Article 10.

This checklist is a starting point written by the build team; it must be reviewed and extended by people with expertise in equity in Indian education before the pilot.

## Always blocking

- Statements or jokes that attribute ability, honesty, cleanliness, intelligence or criminality to a group: gender, caste, religion, region or state, tribe, rural/urban, class or income, language or accent, skin colour, appearance or body size, disability, age.
- Caste markers used as signals of status or character (surnames, occupations described as lowly, purity language).
- Religious or communal framing of scams, crime, danger or untrustworthiness ("messages from X names are junk").
- Rural or poor people as the default scammers, victims or the ignorant ones.
- Gendered roles presented as natural: girls bad at maths/technology, boys not caring, women only cooking, men only earning.
- Fair skin as beauty or quality; darker skin as negative.
- Disability as tragedy, joke or metaphor for failure ("blind to the truth", "crippled filter").

## Check across the whole lesson (advisory unless it also matches the list above)

- Names: a mix across regions, religions and genders; not only North Indian Hindu upper-caste names; not only male protagonists.
- Who is shown as expert, helper, learner, victim: vary by gender and background.
- Family examples: avoid assuming two parents, a car, a private room, a personal phone, English at home. Many learners share a family phone.
- Food, festivals and customs: universal in English source; local specifics only in locale variants (Article 7).
- Regional language and accent: never a marker of being less able.

## How to flag

Quote the text, name the group affected, explain the harm in one sentence, suggest a neutral alternative that keeps the teaching purpose.
