# Analogy sources and structure

Source: constitution Article 7, `docs/04-content-model.md` (analogy blocks), `docs/07-multilingual.md`.

- Map the concept's **relationships**, not its surface. A good test: does the analogy predict how the concept behaves when something changes?
- Universal source domains: the body and senses; food and cooking; home and family; school and exams; weather and nature; maps and journeys; building things; universal childhood games.
- Culture-specific examples (cricket, festivals, particular foods, currency) belong only in locale variants. The structure is universal; examples have locale variants where a local one teaches better.
- Every analogy states where it breaks. Hard concepts get at least two analogies, ideally breaking in different places.
- Where possible, the analogy is interactive and fades into the formal representation (concreteness fading: concrete → bridging → formal).
- In AI lessons, human metaphors ("thinks", "knows", "decides", "learns about you") are used only with their break point shown: say what the machine does not do.

An analogy block has: `concept`, `source`, `mappings` (source element ↔ concept element), `breakPoint`, optional `fading`, `audienceVariants`, `localeVariants`.
